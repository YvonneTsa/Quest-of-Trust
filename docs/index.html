01 / THE DECISION

One score. Four possible actions.

A risk score is only an input. The operating policy chooses what happens next—and that choice affects both the payment and the customer relationship.

The project asks how strategies perform when investigators can only review a limited number of cases per day. It compares a familiar incumbent, readable rules, a logistic baseline, and a combined score under the same synthetic holdout.

Thresholds are selected on earlier dates. The later dates are held out for evaluation. Fraud truth stays separate until after features are built, so the answer key does not become a detection signal.

APPROVEPreserve convenience and margin; accept modeled loss if fraudulent.
CHALLENGEAdd verification friction and a modeled per-event cost.
REVIEWUse a human queue with finite daily capacity.
DECLINEStop the event; risk losing a legitimate customer.
02 / THE FINDINGS

Logistic captures more fraud value and has lower modeled cost across seeds.

Lower modeled net cost is better under the selected assumptions. A negative value is not measured profit; it reflects assumed margin on approved legitimate payments. Good-payment counts exclude planted fraud events.

OBSERVATION 01

More planted fraud value stopped

Across seeds, Logistic captured 90.43% on average versus 73.15% for Rules. Its paired advantage ranged from 14.49 to 21.36 percentage points.

OBSERVATION 02

Less challenge friction, more review work

Logistic challenged a median 3,462 fewer good payments, while sending 214 more good payments to investigator review. This shifts work into the human queue.

OBSERVATION 03

The modeled cost ranking repeated

Logistic had lower modeled cost in all 10 seeds. The median paired difference was −$22,635 per holdout, under the fictional cost and intervention assumptions.

Uncertainty note: the paired Logistic-minus-Rules cost difference ranged from −$25,345 to −$19,903 across 10 seeds (median −$22,635); lower modeled cost occurred in 10/10 runs. These observed simulator ranges are not confidence intervals or proof of real-world performance. Every event, label, and dollar assumption is synthetic.
03 / THE METHOD

From fictional world to an operating choice.

The stages preserve a clear boundary between observable event behavior and the simulator’s hidden answer key.

Default run: 2,000 customers, 800 merchants, 1,200 terminals, and 60 days. Ten sampled attack campaigns are generated for each of four patterns. Each later-date holdout has 350 planted fraud events; the default review limit is 250 cases per day. Seeds and assumptions are documented in the repository.

Stress test, not just a headline

What if capacity or assumptions change?

The dashboard compares precomputed holdout outcomes across daily review limits and fictional cost assumptions. It shows the operating trade-off: cases within the 250-case limit can reach investigators; cases above the limit are sent to challenge.

04 / THE BOUNDARY

What this project can and cannot claim.

The limits matter as much as the findings.

Built to demonstrate

Reproducible simulation, point-in-time feature engineering, temporal evaluation, policy capacity, explicit cost assumptions, and an inspectable analysis pipeline.

  • The population, fraud patterns, labels, and unit economics are fictional.
  • Each later-date holdout has 350 planted fraud events; the ten seeds are repeated draws from the same synthetic design.
  • Observed seed ranges are not confidence intervals, significance tests, or evidence about real payment customers.
  • One account per customer; no device, IP, login, or recovery-event graph.
  • Group rates are descriptive diagnostics, not a fairness certification.
  • The model is an educational baseline, not a production scoring service.
  • Dashboard controls select precomputed scenarios; they do not retrain or rescore events.

Metric note: PR-AUC applies only to strategies with a continuous risk score. The scoreless incumbent is reported as not applicable.
