Fraud is a decision problem.

Quest of Trust studies how a payments team can balance fraud loss, customer friction, review workload, and payment margin—then makes those trade-offs inspectable.

TrustHold is the decision system inside the project.

It generates fictional payment events, builds point-in-time signals, compares a transparent rules score with a logistic baseline, and evaluates actions under capacity and explicit economics.

01 / THE DECISION

One score. Four possible actions.

A risk score is only an input. The operating policy chooses what happens next—and that choice affects both the payment and the customer relationship.

The project asks how strategies perform when investigators can only review a limited number of cases per day. It compares a familiar incumbent, readable rules, a logistic baseline, and a combined score under the same synthetic holdout.

Thresholds are selected on earlier dates. The later dates are held out for evaluation. Fraud truth stays separate until after features are built, so the answer key does not become a detection signal.

APPROVEPreserve convenience and margin; accept modeled loss if fraudulent.
CHALLENGEAdd verification friction and a modeled per-event cost.
REVIEWUse a human queue with finite daily capacity.
DECLINEStop the event; risk losing a legitimate customer.

OBSERVATION 01

More captured value in this holdout

Logistic captures an estimated 81.62% of fraud value versus 78.52% for rules, a 3.10-point difference under assumed action effectiveness.

OBSERVATION 02

Less challenge friction than rules

Logistic sends 143 legitimate events to challenge versus 390 for rules. It sends 14 more legitimate events to review in this run.

OBSERVATION 03

Economics depend on assumptions

The modeled cost advantage is conditional on challenge stop rates, review recovery, attrition, payment margin, and the small synthetic holdout.

Read this carefully: these are simulated outcomes—not evidence about a real processor, customers, savings, fraud prevalence, or production performance. Only 35 fraud events appear in this holdout.
03 / THE METHOD

From fictional world to an operating choice.

The stages preserve a clear boundary between observable event behavior and the simulator’s hidden answer key.

Default run: 200 customers, 80 merchants, 120 terminals, 60 days, and a later-date holdout. The synthetic seed and assumptions are documented in the repository.

04 / THE BOUNDARY

What this portfolio can and cannot claim.

A stronger case study makes its limits as visible as its findings.

Built to demonstrate

Reproducible simulation, point-in-time feature engineering, temporal evaluation, policy capacity, explicit cost assumptions, and an inspectable analysis pipeline.

  • The population, fraud patterns, labels, and unit economics are fictional.
  • The holdout is one simulated period with 35 planted fraud events.
  • One account per customer; no device, IP, login, or recovery-event graph.
  • Group rates are descriptive diagnostics, not a fairness certification.
  • The model is a portfolio baseline, not a production scoring service.
  • Dashboard controls select precomputed scenarios; they do not retrain or rescore events.

Metric-quality note: the generated Markdown report currently repeats the Rules PR-AUC value in the scoreless Incumbent row. This portfolio page omits that invalid comparison; a strategy without a continuous risk score has no applicable PR-AUC.
